Thesis

NEXARIS: Imbalanced Multi-Label Node Classification on Evolving Graphs

Defended, A+

BSc Thesis, IIUC, 2025 to 2026 · Lead researcher, with Umme Kawsher, supervised by Subrina Akter

Benchmarked across four domains: social networks, citation networks, computational biology, and financial transactions. Outperformed standard GCN baselines by 5 to 9 pts Micro/Macro-F1.

Why it was needed

Real-world graphs rarely hold still and rarely have balanced labels: fraud is rare, minority classes are rare, and new nodes keep arriving. Most node classification work assumes static, balanced graphs, which quietly breaks in deployment. NEXARIS was built to hold up under both problems at once, and the financial-transaction results showed it staying robust even as the underlying graph drifted, which matters for anyone trying to use graph learning outside a clean benchmark.

Published

KNOW-NET: A Knowledge-Aware Cross-Attention Network with NER for Fake News Classification

IEEE QPAIN 2026

Lead author, with Umme Kawsher and Zinnia Sultana · Presented 16 to 18 April 2026, CUET, Chattogram

A dual-encoder architecture fusing transformer-based text with knowledge-graph embeddings through cross-attention, regularized with an auxiliary named-entity-recognition task. Reached 98.55% F1 / accuracy, a 1.41 point gain over prior state of the art (p < 0.0001), at 15ms per sample.

Why it was needed

Text-only fake news detectors miss a lot: a false claim can be phrased exactly like a true one. Grounding the model in a knowledge graph gives it something outside the sentence itself to check the claim against, which is closer to how a human fact-checker actually works. The NER regularization also pushed the model to pay attention to the entities that carry the claim's meaning rather than surface wording, which is most of why it outperformed the prior benchmark instead of just matching it.

A Novel Association Rule Mining Approach Based on Bio-Inspired Algorithms for Air Quality Analysis in Dhaka, Bangladesh

IEEE QPAIN 2025

Contributing author, with Subrina Akter, Yamina Islam, Mohammad Shamsul Arefin, and Ahmed Wasif Reza · Presented 31 July to 2 August 2025, BAUST, Rangpur

Co-designed a hybrid Genetic Algorithm plus Particle Swarm Optimization approach to replace exhaustive candidate generation in association rule mining. Delivered 87% lower memory use and higher rule quality than Apriori (p = 0.0002).

Why it was needed

Exhaustive rule mining (Apriori) becomes impractical fast as a dataset grows, exactly the kind of dataset Dhaka's air quality monitoring produces. Replacing exhaustive search with a bio-inspired search meant the same pollution-pattern analysis that would have needed far more memory and time could run practically, which matters if this kind of analysis is ever going to run routinely rather than as a one-off research exercise.

Cross-Project Early Detection of Contributor Disengagement in the NumFocus Open-Source Community

Under review

Lead author

The first reproducible benchmark for predicting contributor disengagement across a large multi-project open-source dataset, with a leakage-aware, project-grouped evaluation protocol. Best model reached an AUC of ~0.79 (p < 10⁻⁷).

Why it was needed

Open-source projects lose contributors quietly and usually notice only after they're gone, by which point there's nothing left to do. A benchmark that can flag disengagement early gives maintainers a chance to actually intervene, but only if the evaluation is honest about generalizing to projects it hasn't seen, which is exactly what the project-grouped protocol was built to guarantee against the more common (and more flattering) leaky alternative.

Road Surface Damage Classification (MS-AttNet)

Under review

Second author

A lightweight CNN for road surface damage classification on the RSD-BD dataset. Developed the core architecture and led implementation and result analysis, reaching 93%+ accuracy at a fraction of the parameter cost of standard CNN backbones.

Why it was needed

Road damage detection is most useful exactly where compute is scarcest, on-device in a municipal inspection vehicle rather than a data center. A heavy CNN backbone defeats the purpose even if it's slightly more accurate. Keeping the parameter count low while holding accuracy above 93% is what actually makes this deployable rather than just publishable.

Bengali Depression Detection from Social Media Text

Under review

Mentor

Mentored study design and evaluation protocol end to end. The final model reached 90.5% weighted F1 on a 21,900-post dataset.

Why it was needed

Almost all mental-health text classification research is built for English. Bengali speakers showing signs of depression on social media are effectively invisible to that body of work. A dataset and model built specifically for Bengali is a step toward the kind of early, language-appropriate screening tools that could actually reach that population.

In progress

COMPASS: Cross-Context Generalization of Outbreak Surge Forecasting Models

Implementation in progress

Lead researcher

Tests whether outbreak-surge prediction models trained in a high-resource health system generalize to a low-resource one, using US and Bangladesh measles surveillance data with a lag-1 forecasting design.

Why it matters

Most outbreak forecasting models are trained and validated in wealthy health systems with clean, complete surveillance data, then quietly assumed to work anywhere. COMPASS tests that assumption directly against Bangladesh's measles data, which is exactly the kind of lower-resource setting where a model failing silently would matter most.

OFA-Seis: Optimized Fusion Attention with Physics-Guided Multi-Task Learning for Seismic Inversion

Manuscript in preparation

Sole author · Preliminary results presented at the Scholars' Convergence Research and Innovation Summit 2026, CUSS, 22 January 2026

An explainable, physics-guided deep learning framework combining cross-attention and multi-task learning with geophysical constraints, aimed at improving both accuracy and interpretability of subsurface imaging.

Why it matters

Pure deep-learning approaches to seismic inversion can produce results that look plausible but violate basic physics, which geophysicists rightly distrust. Building the physical constraints directly into the model, rather than hoping the network learns them implicitly, is what makes the output something a geophysicist could actually act on.
