StudyDesk

A Django study management platform for class scheduling, note organization, and goal tracking, with AI-generated quizzes: any uploaded PDF becomes a five-question multiple-choice quiz, scored instantly.

Why it was needed

Students collect PDFs and lecture notes faster than they ever revisit them, and end up juggling a separate app each for scheduling, to-do lists, and file storage on top of that. StudyDesk puts class scheduling, note organization, and goal tracking under one roof, and then closes the gap most students actually fall into: turning a document into a scored quiz on the spot, right where the material already lives, rather than leaving it un-tested until exam week.

Urban Gardening Assistant

An AI application that plans crop schedules with a genetic algorithm, recommends companion plants through constraint satisfaction, and identifies pest issues with simulated annealing, wrapped in a Django app with three chatbot-style guides.

Why it was needed

Urban growers usually have no formal training in crop rotation, companion planting, or pest management, and get it wrong through trial and error on a small, unforgiving plot. Three different optimization problems (scheduling, pairing, diagnosis) each needed a different algorithm rather than one model stretched to cover all three, which is why the project uses three.

TSN-Simulation

An OMNeT++/INET simulation of a Time-Sensitive Networking topology, paired with a Python regression pipeline that predicts network delay from simulation data, including a live prediction bridge called from inside the running simulation.

Why it was needed

Time-Sensitive Networking exists to guarantee low, predictable latency for industrial and automotive systems, so being able to predict delay from live queue and traffic conditions, rather than only measuring it after the fact, is what would let a real deployment react instead of just report.

Advanced Task Scheduler

A CPU scheduling simulator exposing FCFS, SJF, Priority, and a time-sliced Round Robin algorithm through a small API, returning start, finish, waiting, and turnaround time for every submitted task.

Algorithms and simulations

Root-Finding Algorithms Visualizer

An interactive teaching tool animating five root-finding methods, Bisection, Newton-Raphson, Secant, Regula Falsi, and Fixed-Point Iteration, with a live function chart, a convergence-error chart, and a step-by-step iteration table.

Why it was needed

Root-finding methods are usually taught as static formulas on a whiteboard, which hides the thing that actually differentiates them: how fast and how reliably each one converges. Watching the convergence-error chart update per iteration makes that difference visible instead of theoretical, which is exactly what a numerical methods student needs.

Journey Planner

A console route planner modeling ten real locations around Chittagong as a weighted graph, using Dijkstra's algorithm for the shortest path and depth-first search to enumerate every possible route between two points.

Why it was needed

Shortest-path algorithms are easy to demo on toy graphs but harder to make feel real. Building it on ten actual named locations around Chittagong, with realistic distances, turns the algorithm from an abstract graph exercise into something that answers a genuine "how do I get there" question.

Maze Escape

A 3D maze game rendered in OpenGL, with a new maze carved on every run using randomized recursive backtracking, guaranteed solvable through a breadth-first search that also keeps obstacles off the valid path.

Why it was needed

A randomly generated maze with randomly placed obstacles can easily become unsolvable by accident. Running BFS at generation time to compute the guaranteed path, then keeping obstacles off it, is what makes every generated maze fair, rather than occasionally impossible.
